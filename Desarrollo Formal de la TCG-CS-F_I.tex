\begin{abstract}
The \textbf{Quantum Measurement Problem} in Quantum Mechanics lacks an internal dynamic mechanism for state reduction, traditionally requiring an external postulate. This work presents the formal development of the objective collapse mechanism embedded in the \textbf{TCG-CS-F (Constitutive Theory of Gravity with Physical Screening Field)}. We argue that the non-linear coupling of the scalar field $\chi$ with baryonic matter, defined by the screening potential $\mathbf{V(\chi) = M^4/\chi}$, induces a gravitational self-interaction that forces the decoherence of massive systems. The model's consistency is demonstrated by the \textbf{analytical derivation} of an exact critical mass threshold: $\mathbf{M_{cr} \approx 10^9 \text{ amu}}$. Experimental verification of this threshold through macromolecular interferometry would confirm that modified gravity is the unifying agent for both quantum and cosmological phenomenology.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction: The Need for Objective Collapse}

Quantum Mechanics (QM) has been validated with exceptional precision, yet its fundamental interpretation clashes with observed macroscopic reality. The Schrödinger equation is linear and unitary, permitting superpositions of macroscopic states (like Schrödinger's cat) that are never observed at the human scale. This \textbf{Quantum Crisis} demands that the transition from the probabilistic to the classical world be explained by intrinsic physical dynamics, rather than by the "magical" intervention of an observer or *ad hoc* projection postulates.

\textbf{TCG-CS-F} offers an "endogenous" solution. Since the theory already unifies the dark sector (Dark Matter and Dark Energy) and satisfies Solar System constraints via a scalar field of modified gravity ($\chi$) with a screening mechanism, it is natural to postulate that this same physical field is responsible for breaking quantum linearity.

This work is dedicated to the detailed mathematical derivation of the key prediction introduced preliminarily in previous works: the \textbf{Objective Collapse Threshold ($\mathbf{M_{cr}}$)}. We demonstrate that this threshold is not a free parameter but an inevitable consequence of the theory's Lagrangian structure.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{A. $\chi$-Induced Decoherence Dynamics}{A. chi-Induced Decoherence Dynamics}} 

We consider a particle of mass $M$ in a spatial superposition of two states centered at $x$ and $x'$, separated by a distance $\Delta x = |x - x'|$. The evolution of the reduced density matrix $\rho(x, x', t)$ follows a Lindblad-type master equation, dominated by an off-diagonal damping term:

\begin{equation}
    \frac{\partial \rho(x, x', t)}{\partial t} = -\frac{i}{\hbar} [H, \rho] - \Gamma(x, x') \rho(x, x', t)
\end{equation}

In TCG-CS-F, the decoherence rate $\Gamma$ arises from the differential gravitational self-energy between the two superposition states, mediated by the scalar field. Approximating for small displacements compared to the field's effective Compton wavelength:

\begin{equation}
    \Gamma \approx \frac{1}{\hbar} \left( \Delta E_{\text{Newt}} + \Delta E_{\chi} \right)
\end{equation}

The term $\Delta E_{\chi}$ is the scalar field contribution. Given the potential $V(\chi) \sim 1/\chi$ and coupling $\alpha$, the non-linear interaction scales with the object's density $\rho_{obj}$. The characteristic decoherence time is defined as $\tau_{D} = 1/\Gamma$.

Analytically, we find that the decoherence rate scales with mass as $\Gamma \propto M^{5/3}$ in the transition regime (a stronger dependence than the $M^2$ of standard gravity due to the screening form factor).
